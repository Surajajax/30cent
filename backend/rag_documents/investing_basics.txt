INVESTING BASICS

Investing involves putting money into assets with the expectation that they may increase in value or generate income over time.

COMMON INVESTMENTS

Common investment categories include:

- Stocks
- Bonds
- Mutual funds
- Exchange-traded funds
- Real estate

STOCKS

A stock represents an ownership interest in a company.

The value of a stock can increase or decrease based on market conditions and other factors.

BONDS

A bond generally represents a loan made by an investor to a borrower such as a company or government.

Bonds can provide interest payments, although their values can also change.

MUTUAL FUNDS

A mutual fund pools money from multiple investors and invests it in a portfolio of assets according to the fund's strategy.

EXCHANGE-TRADED FUNDS

An exchange-traded fund, or ETF, is an investment fund whose shares trade on an exchange.

RISK AND RETURN

Investment returns are uncertain.

Investments can lose value, and the amount of risk can vary between different types of investments.

Generally, assets with greater potential returns may also involve greater risk.

DIVERSIFICATION

Diversification means spreading investments across different assets, companies, industries, or other categories.

Diversification can reduce dependence on the performance of a single investment, although it cannot eliminate investment risk.

TIME HORIZON

Investment decisions can depend on how long the money can remain invested.

Short-term financial needs may require different approaches from long-term financial goals.

RISK TOLERANCE

Risk tolerance refers to how much investment loss or fluctuation a person can financially and emotionally withstand.

An investor should consider their financial goals, time horizon, and ability to tolerate losses.

LONG-TERM INVESTING

Long-term investing generally focuses on financial goals that are several years or more into the future.

Examples include retirement, education, or long-term wealth-building goals.

IMPORTANT

Investing involves risk, including the possibility of losing money.

General investing information does not replace personalized financial advice.
